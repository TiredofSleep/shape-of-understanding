# Sentences to check: a reread of the book, 2026-11-16

An outside read of this repository (the grounding handoff of 2026-11-16) found three sentences whose mathematics
was off while every script passed, because the errors were in prose, not in computations. Those three are fixed
(below). It also asked for one reread of the whole book for sentences of the same kind, words like *by
definition*, *always*, *exactly* or *is the* with no check behind them, and for a **list for the author, not
silent rewrites**. This is that list. Nothing in it has been changed in the book.

**How it was made.** A fresh reader, with no stake in the book, read all of
`the_shape_of_understanding_BOOK.md` in order and checked each candidate, by computation where a number was
involved. Every quotation below was then compared with the book's text, and the computations behind items 13, 17
and 18 were rerun independently (siamese starts, Fourier overshoot, the helper's gain). `curriculum_checks.py`
passes all 107 checks, so every item is a gap the scripts never test.

## Already fixed (2026-11-16)

- **§1.6b, the centroid.** It said the centroid "by definition" minimizes the total distance to the points. It
  minimizes the total *squared* distance; for equidistant points symmetry makes it the nearest point as well. A
  lopsided triangle, (0, 0), (6, 0), (0, 1), has centroid total 8.15 and best total 6.88. The wording is fixed in
  §1.6b, Chapter 2's foundation summary, the Coda and the README, and `curriculum_checks.py` now checks the
  lopsided case, which the old wording fails.
- **Chapter 6, Mertens.** The sieve's product over the primes up to √N behaves like 2e^(−γ)/ln N, about 1.12/ln N,
  not like 1/ln N: it stays high by the factor 2e^(−γ) ≈ 1.123 forever. Now said, and checked.
- **Chapter 7, the real part.** "The real part is the observable" was wrong. Neither part alone is observable;
  magnitudes and relative phases are. The misconception box now agrees, and a check shows a common phase turn
  changing the real part and no probability.

## The list

### False as written

1. **L69–70, L172–173.** "Get four toothpicks and some gumdrops…"; "four toothpicks the same length will not lie
   flat". Four gumdrops all one toothpick apart need C(4, 2) = 6 toothpicks, and four equal toothpicks lie flat as
   a square. *Suggested:* "four gumdrops and six toothpicks"; "four gumdrops, every pair one toothpick apart, will
   not lie flat".
2. **L677–679.** Decibels, pH, stellar magnitudes and the Richter scale: "each equal step is one more doubling".
   None is a doubling: one pH or Richter unit is ×10 (Richter energy ×31.6), 10 dB is ×10 in power, one magnitude
   is ×2.512. *Suggested:* "each equal step multiplies by the same factor".
3. **L666–668.** "…cooling, and diffusion all obey dy/dt ∝ y, and the solution is always e to a rate times
   time". Diffusion obeys ∂u/∂t = D ∂²u/∂x²; a point source's centre decays as t^(−1/2), a power law.
   *Suggested:* drop diffusion, or say each Fourier mode of a diffusing profile decays as e^(−Dk²t).
4. **L356–357.** "The unique function equal to its own derivative is e^x". Every Ce^x is, 0 included.
   *Suggested:* "…and equal to 1 at 0". (L694's "unique base" is right.)
5. **L317–318.** "the deep algebras of mathematics have sizes that are always powers of two". The 3 × 3 matrices
   have dimension 9, so(3) has 3, G₂ 14, E₈ 248. *Suggested:* "the Clifford algebras (and ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆) have sizes
   that are powers of two".
6. **L846–848** (also Fig. 7 and L1123). Complex eigenvalues are said to make the map "a pure rotation". The
   converse fails: [[1, −1], [1, 1]] has eigenvalues 1 ± i and spirals outward; [[1, −2], [1, −1]] has eigenvalues
   ±i and is not orthogonal. *Suggested:* "eigenvalues re^(±iθ): a turn by θ with a stretch by r, in a possibly
   slanted frame; a pure rotation only when |λ| = 1 and the matrix is orthogonal".
7. **L853–856.** "a transformation that rotates in several independent planes… lives in… the quaternions, where
   the order of the rotations matters". Rotations in independent planes commute. Order matters for successive
   turns about different axes. *Suggested:* "turns about different axes, one after another, live in ℍ".
8. **L1066–1073** (honest failures). "every attempt to find the 1/3 in a physical law… misses… vanishes in every
   force law". Quark charges are −1/3 and +2/3; gas pressure P = ⅓ρ⟨v²⟩ and radiation pressure P = u/3 come from
   ⟨cos²θ⟩ = 1/3; the dipole's 3cos²θ − 1 vanishes at cos²θ = 1/3, the magic angle the book cites at L1255.
   *Suggested, keeping the picture:* the universality dies; where physics shows a 1/3, it is this same
   three-direction geometry showing through.

### Overstated

9. **L299–307, L313, L1084.** "Every old piece becomes two… the total number of pieces… doubles." Only if the
   empty piece counts: counting dots, sticks and faces by hand gives 7 → 15 (2^N − 1). §2.3 counts "the empty
   choice"; §1.1b does not. *Suggested:* count the empty choice too, the piece that, joining, becomes the new
   gumdrop.
10. **L329–334, Fig. 2, L1240.** "five-fold symmetry cannot tile the plane — a theorem". True for repeating
    tilings (lattices) only; Penrose tilings and quasicrystals have five-fold order. *Suggested:* "no repeating
    pattern can have five-fold symmetry".
11. **L523–528.** "A thing that squares to −1 is exactly a quarter-turn… Every later appearance of i… is this same
    candy face-plane". In Cl(3) the volume e₁e₂e₃ also squares to −1 and commutes with everything: it is the
    Pauli matrices' scalar i, not a plane. *Suggested:* "each face-plane squares to −1 and turns its own plane a
    quarter; the volume e₁e₂e₃ is another i".
12. **L889–890.** The group axioms listed are closure, identity and inverse; associativity is missing (also in the
    D₃ check). *Suggested:* add "and associativity, automatic for moves".
13. **L926–928.** "place 1, step up-and-right…" never says where 1 goes. From an arbitrary start the method makes
    a magic square from only 1 of 9 cells at order 3, 5 of 25 at order 5 and 7 of 49 at order 7 (rerun).
    *Suggested:* "place 1 in the middle of the top row".
14. **L660–661.** "only five pairs {p, q} satisfy 1/p + 1/q > 1/2". {2, q} satisfies it for every q.
    *Suggested:* "with p, q ≥ 3".
15. **L753, L1305–1306.** "Because evolution is rotation, and rotation is i", and "Not one of them is a fact of
    physics". Real orthogonal evolution also rotates; real-number quantum theory matches every single-system
    prediction and fails only in networks with independent sources (Renou et al., *Nature* 2021, tested 2022).
    *Suggested:* "complex numbers are the natural language of turning; that nature needs them is a subtler,
    experimental fact".
16. **L801–805, L820, L141–142.** "Pythagoras: his theorem, and the crisis it caused". The relation and
    √2 ≈ 1.414213 are on Old Babylonian tablets (Plimpton 322, YBC 7289, about 1800 BCE), and the discovery of
    incommensurability is traditionally Hippasus's, the name in the title of the von Fritz paper the book cites.
    *Suggested:* "the theorem that carries his name… the crisis his school uncovered".
17. **L1383–1386.** "a helper that is unsure always gains… and gains more the less sure it is". The script checks
    Gaussian beliefs only. A helper unsure only of *how much* its action helps gains nothing: a belief uniform on
    [1, 2] gains 0, one uniform on [−1, 2] gains 0.167 (rerun). *Suggested:* "a helper unsure whether its action
    helps or harms…".
18. **L865–868.** "Any repeating shape is a sum of pure rotations… a seven-term sum already matches it closely".
    True on average. Near the jump the partial sums peak at 1.179 however many terms (1.1807, 1.179, 1.179 at 7,
    50, 500; rerun): Gibbs's overshoot of about 9% of the jump. *Suggested:* "close on average; near a jump the
    circles always overshoot by about 9% of the jump".
19. **Smaller slips.**
    - L1139, "One clean null… falsifies": a null counts only if the study was powered for it.
    - L595, "vertex angle (cos = −1/3)": −1/3 is the central angle's cosine; a face's vertex angle is 60°.
    - L822, "§1.1's segment doubled": the diagonal is √2 times the side; only its square doubles.
    - L1202–1203, x → 2/x: the swap holds for positive fractions only.
    - L1338, "the one shape that has no edge": every smooth closed surface has none, and L1374 calls the ring
      "edgeless too".

### Unchecked

20. **L305–307, L1006–1007**, the "(verified…)" labels: the checks are tautologies (`2**(N + 1) == 2 * 2**N`,
    `4**n == 2**(2*n)`). They count no faces and compute no Clifford dimension. The claims are true; the label is
    not earned. *Suggested:* count Cl(n)'s blades and the nonempty faces.
21. **L246–247, L1429, L1565.** "Every mathematical claim in the book is reproduced by running them." Items 1–8,
    10 and 12–14 have no check, and the three wordings disagree (claim / computable fact / assertion).
    *Suggested:* add the checks, then use L1429's narrower wording everywhere.
22. **L756–757.** "[Feynman 1965… 'the arrow that rotates']". The rotating arrows are from *QED* (1985, already in
    the references); 1965 matches no listed Feynman work, and the quoted phrase could not be confirmed.
    *Suggested:* "[Feynman 1985, *QED*, draws this as turning arrows]", without quotation marks.
23. **L908.** "For a square-free N" is needless: the first number sharing a factor with N is its smallest prime
    factor for every N > 1.
